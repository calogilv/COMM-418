# [Who can afford to get ahead in America?](https://www.nbcnews.com/specials/unaffordable-america-economy-inflation-us-jobs-prices-bills-stocks/) #
By Shannon Pettypiece

Published by NBC News

This story falls into the category of stories explaining something in a certain number of graphs. The beginning of the article sets up the context for a graph of the top 1% of earners' gains over the past 26 years compared to the bottom 50% of earners' gains over that time. Rich people are multiplying their wealth and buying up assets while poorer people are have a difficult time buying items and getting a job, Pettypiece writes. The graph of the top 1%'s earnings and the bottom 50%'s earnings is then shown.

That all makes sense and displays the proper context. But after that, the article is an onslaught of nine additional graphs about the economy. They do transition well from one another, in that the a graph about different investment portfolio rates' growths comes right after the graph of the top and bottom earners, showing *how* some earn more and some earn less. And then there's an inflation graph, a graph with the prices of common groceries, a housing and income graph, a graph of the average electric bill costs, a graph of unemployment, a graph of job openings, a graph of jobs being added and removed and a graph showing the results of a poll that asked people to rate the economy.

When put together, the graphs do transition well from one another and can tell a story. But I wish there was a little more text, reporting and context given about the graphs and what caused the changes shown on the graph. The article forces the reader to interpret data instead of offering an interpretation of the data. 

The article also features striking quotes from eight everyday people, though, which I loved. I felt it added an element of humanity to the story. It makes the story an almost perfect 50/50 split of data and human's stories.
