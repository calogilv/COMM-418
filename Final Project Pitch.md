In Fort Worth, Texas, vehicles crashed into 448 people near bus stops between 2018 and 2022, according to a [2025 analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001457525002234).

How often are vehicles crashing into people near WMATA bus stops?

Combining a dataset from the Metropolitan Police Department and the District Department of Transportation can answer that question.

The DDOT's ["Crashes in DC" dataset](https://opendata.dc.gov/datasets/DCGIS::crashes-in-dc/about) lists every reported crash that has occured within Washington, D.C., proportedly since 1975. The dataset includes cardinal coordinates for each crash and the CrimeID for each crash.

A separate dataset, titled "Crash Details Table" on OpenDataDC, lists what type of vehicles were involved in each crash and that same CrimeID the "Crashes in DC" dataset uses. Thereby, it's possible to see which crashes involve busses and where those crashes take place. Additional datasets will need to be used to include WMATA crashes in Virginia and Maryland. 

By mapping crash data on top of data of WMATA bus stop locations, [which has its own database](https://opendata.dc.gov/datasets/e85b5321a5a84ff9af56fd614dab81b3_53/explore?location=38.923580%2C-77.046055%2C10), the public can learn which bus stops may be dangerous, obviously noting that correlation does not mean causation as relevant.

This story would also contextualize specific bus stops which some transit enthusiasts have characterized as dangerous. In a Jan. 29 video, YouTube user "Trains Are Awesome" highlighted a bus stop that he thought is the worst: one on the George Washington Memorial Parkway surrounded by hilly forest and rushing cars. Simirarly, transit enthusiasts [voted](https://www.wbur.org/hereandnow/2016/08/29/bus-stops) the Route 29 and Crestmoor Drive bus stop as the worst bus stop in America in 2016. Reporting this would possibly show how bad, or not bad, these bus stops are.
