# Chart: US to overwhelmingly build clean power in 2026 #
By Julian Spactor

[https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/clean-energy/chart-us-overwhelmingly-build-clean-power](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/clean-energy/chart-us-overwhelmingly-build-clean-power)

This is a fairly simple data story reporting on the U.S. Energy Information Administration releasing a report of which power plants developers plan to construct in 2026. Fifty-one percent of the planned projects for the year are solar-based, 28% are energy battery constructions and 14% are wind projects. The Canary Media article lists the rest of the types of project types expected in 2026 and the political contexts around them. The journalist also highlights the fact that the projects will add 86 gigawattes of power in a year, a record high.

Julian Spactor does about everything one should do in a basic story summarizing a data release: highlights what the data says about the present moment or future and provides context (that being the U.S. will get the biggest boost to its energy grid ever this year). He also compares the share of planned power plant types to the share of current power plant types, which is important and shows that gas is still the most popular type.

The article makes use of a donut graph, which I find similar to a pie chart, relying on people's ability to assess area. A bar graph might have been more perferable. The Energy Information Administration [analysis](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67205) also uses a stacked bar graph to show when in 2026 different types of energy plants will be built, which the journalism article ommitted. I feel like that graph would've been beneficial to include.
